S2 PID axiomatic background
This appendix briefly reviews the axiomatic language of partial information decomposition (PID), which will be used to justify the correspondence between PEID and PID in the three-variable case.
For two source variables X1, X2 and one target variable Y , the joint mutual information is decomposed as
I(X1, X2;Y ) = Red(X1, X2;Y ) +Un(X1;Y | X2) +Un(X2;Y | X1) + Syn(X1, X2;Y ). (S20)
Here Red(·) denotes the redundancy term, while the unique and synergistic terms are induced by the redundancy function. In this paper, we only use the following standard axioms.
Symmetry (S). The redundancy is invariant under any permutation π of the source indices {1, . . . , k}:
Red(A1, . . . , Ak; S) = Red(Aπ(1), . . . , Aπ(k); S). (S21)
Self-redundancy (I). For a single source, redundancy reduces to ordinary mutual information:
Red(A; S) = I(A; S). (S22)
Monotonicity (M). Redundancy cannot increase when more sources are included:
Red(A1, . . . , Ak; S) ≤ Red(A1, . . . , Ak−1; S). (S23)
Identity (Id). When the target is the union of the two sources,
Red(A1, A2; A1 ∪ A2) = I(A1; A2). (S24)
Left chain rule (LC). For two target variables S and S′ and source variables A1, . . . , Ak, the redundancy associated with the joint target (S, S′) satisfies
Red(A1, . . . , Ak; S, S′) = Red(A1, . . . , Ak; S) + Red(A1, . . . , Ak; S′ | S). (S25)

Under these axioms, the unique term is determined by

S3 Proofs of the PEID properties
This section establishes the principal theoretical properties of PEID used in the main text. We first prove that, for two independently intervened source variables, the PID redundancy vanishes and integrated effective information reduces to two-source synergy. We then prove the non-negativity and upper bound of integrated effective information for an arbitrary non-empty source set. Finally, we establish its hierarchical additivity under recursive binary partitioning. Unless stated otherwise, A, B ⊆ V denote non-empty source and target index sets, and all information quantities are evaluated under the common intervention-induced distribution ql(xt , xt+l).
